virotherapy is the technique of using lab-made specific viruses to target diseases like cancer. cancer cells kind of become invisible to immune cells. if virus is inserted into cancerous cells, they die off due to the virus and become less effective against the immune cell. so there are three parties - cancer cells, virus and the immune cells, each fighting against each other. 

there is a safety-progress tradeoff in medical science. if there are less regulations on what can be tested on humans, then there will be more experiments, more information and therefore faster progress. but the people undergoing the experiments will be harmed. on the other hand, if there are many regulations and human experimentation is difficult then progress will be slower but on the other hand less humans will be harmed. currently the regulations lean far on the safety side. which i don't think is the optimal. but i think it does become optimal when you assign high weights to the life of current humans and low weights to the life of future humans. 

if someone's model assigns a little more weight to the lives of future humans then it makes sense to speed up medical progress at the cost of some damage to current human lives.

but then again if i had to choose between a conventional and an experimental therapy, its obvious which one i would choose.
