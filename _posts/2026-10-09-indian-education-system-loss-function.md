artificial neural networks are high-parameter functions that are trained to minimise the training error which is based on a loss function. the loss function measures the output of the model relative to the true output. the network updates its weights and biases using gradient descent to minimise the loss function.

the indian education system has a loss function based on grades and grades only. the only thing that matters is how much you score. on paper. human brains are wired for social validation, especially in teenage. so the teenage brain, in order to minimise its training error - focuses on maximising grades and grades only, anyway possible.

indians are still poor. and what majority of them value most is safety and certainty. a stable salary. i'm at a college doing a 'placement-oriented' course. the loss function here is to get a placement. so people optimise for that. but i think there are enough smart, young people here that they could build something valuable togethor. but i don't think that has happened yet. and my hypothesis is that given the limited time, majority of the students want financial security first. 

i think this ability of being free of financial worries is a superpower for the west. students there probably don't have to worry as much about financial safety as the indian students. they have more bandwidth to try things. im guessing really. my brain is kinda sad that there's no product-building at such a top indian college. i'm definitely missing a lot of factors.
