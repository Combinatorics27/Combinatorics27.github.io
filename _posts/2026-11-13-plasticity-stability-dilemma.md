human brains are plastic. plastic means moldable. humans can learn new things and form new memories. that ability is plasticity. 

stability is the maintainance of your identity. even though humans can learn new things, they stay similar to their past selves.

human brain is made of 80 billion neurons and 100 trillion synpases. despite sounding so large, they are finite. which means that there is a physical limit to how much a single human can learn. 

the plasticity-stability dilemma is the tradeoff between the ability of the brain to learn something new while also ensuring that the stability of past memories and knowledge is preserved.

think of neurons like islands and synapses like bridges between them. you have a fixed number of islands and bridges. learning something new is like relocating the bridges between the islands. you want to be able to do that while also ensuring that not much of the prior arrangement is disturbed. 

the dilemma now also comes into play in AI. some AI models exhibit what is known as catastrophic forgetting. catastrophic forgetting is the phenomeneon where when a model is trained on some new dataset, it updates its weights but in that process, the new weights now don't perform well for the old dataset that it had learnt from.
