date: 2026-08-27

the human brain is wired according to relative fitness. what matters is not how well you do, but how well you do relative to your peers. so the brain keeps gathering information about the fitness of peers and compares its fitness against the peers.

but the issue with this strategy is that you're constrained by the data your brain has. your reference point can only be people you know the fitness of. 

colleges are closed environments. you compete with your batchmates. and because of the regularity, what stays on the top of your mind is your performance compared to your peers. this works for many cases but fails in some. it fails when your incentives do not align with the incentives of major of your peers. because competitions are rewarding. so your brain will want to compete with the peers forgetting that the race you are running has a prize you never wanted in the first place. 

it is then a good practice to have a 'value constitution' which you read periodically to remind your brain what exactly you intend to do, so that evolutionary biology doesn't take over.
