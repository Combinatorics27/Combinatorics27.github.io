consider a person staying at a village. and another person staying in new york city. their work ends at 5pm and they want to use their leisure time to do something fun.

the person living in the village has only one option - go to the cricket ground and play with the dozen other people. the person in the new york, well can literally do anything. the city provides practically unlimited options. 

the villager goes and plays cricket each day, strengthening the bond with other players and comes to love the playing.

the new yorker does fun stuff too, but their brain sometimes wanders off thinking about the other potentially more fun activities they could instead be doing.

in economics we call this opportunity cost. in biology one might call it relative fitness. 

the absolute fun value of a task remains the same, what changes is the value of other tasks one could be doing. 

Red queen hypothesis refers to the fact that species evolve and become better but because other competing species become better as well, both species are now better in absolute terms but nothing changes relatively. 

the human version of this is easterlin paradox. at a given time, rich people are definitely happier than the poorer people in their country, but over a long term, the rising average income in a country doesn't lead to rising average happiness. happiness it turns out is relative to how you perform compared to your peers. 

a lower quantile income person in the 2026 has access to technology and healthcare that wasn't available to the richest 1% 200-300 years ago, but is definitely not as much happy. 

i've thought of this before but i don't know the solution. biology wires us to keep pushing ourselves. to stay ahead of our peers. but if we stop and look, the fact is that we've so much more abundance and wealth compared to even the richest in the past. 

so i think long term happiness and content can come from making oneself realise this fact.
