the bitter lesson refers to experts in AI wanting to instill human like knowledge in systems despite knowing that past techniques like such have been beaten by just scaling search and learning through compute. 

in the short term, compute is limited, so experts try to give systems human-gathered knowledge in order to make them perform better. this works but then is easily dominated by systems that just brute force learning through more compute.

maybe this is what's happening with current mathematicians. maybe we are wrong to believe that human understanding of mathematics is special and useful. maybe ai solving papers that despite not feeling intuitive, will lead to a better world.

the dutch disease refers to an economic situation where a country suddenly finds huge amounts of natural resources. they export it, which leads to their currency appreciating, which leads to their export of other products becoming less competetive outside of their country but also since imports become cheaper, they become less competetive within the country as well.

this causes degradation of other industries. what also occurs is resource movement which is the movement of labour and other resources from other industries to the natural resource industry in order to maximise short term earnings.

its not a law or rule, but an observation. useful as a mental model maybe.
