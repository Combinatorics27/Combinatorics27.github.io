conditioning in neuroscience refers to the phenomenon where when some cues are bundled togethor to produce some behaviour, the brain learns to associate both cues with the behaviour, even though only one cue is natural.

consider the conventional example of a dog. a dog salivates when he sees food. the owner starts ringing a bell to call the dog whenever its time to eat. the dog's brain slowly is conditioned to then salivate also when the bell rings. after the conditioning, the dog will salivate when the bell rings even in cases where there's no food along with the ringing. 

in techincal terms, seeing food is unconditional stimulus, the dog's brain is programmed by evolution to do that. a bell ringing is neutral stimulus in that to a normal dog, it wouldn't make him salivate. but to a conditioned dog, the neutral stimulus becomes a conditional stimulus.

interoceptive conditioning is when one's body knowledge - such as heartbeat, lack of breath, etc. turns into a conditioned stimulus. the brain learns to associate these bodily signs to stress or anxiety even though the increased heartrate or lack of breath could have been caused by something non-serious.
