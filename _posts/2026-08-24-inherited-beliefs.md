for a couple of years, i stayed with rajasthanis/gujratis/jain/marwadis. less than 5% were non-vegetarian. or atleast publicly non-vegetarian. many of them wouldn't even go to places that had non-veg options on the menu. non-veg was a sin. a disgusting thing.  the hostel mess was purely vegetarian and always had jain options as well.

i'm now at a college in chennai. eggs and chicken and fish abound in the mess. the demographics have reversed. most here are non-vegetarian. 

the not-so-novel-everyone-knows-already discovery is that the state you were born in and your dietary preference are not independent. rajasthan and gujarat are majorly vegetarian, whereas west bengal is majorly non-vegetarian. 

whats more interesting is the inference. dietary preferences, and the reason for them, are inherited. the brain accepts, without questioning, whatever beliefs and values we grow up in.

kind of like [gellman amnesia](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Gell-Mann_Amnesia_effect). my hypothesis is that this happens because critical thinking is resource intensive for the brain, so the brain learns, during childhood, what parents and peers are doing, and most of the time, accepts those things as true.
