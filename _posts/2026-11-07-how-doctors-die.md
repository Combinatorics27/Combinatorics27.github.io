[how doctors die](https://web.archive.org/web/20191114023829/https://www.saturdayeveningpost.com/2013/03/how-doctors-die/) is an article by a doctor who notes the difference between how normal people die and how doctors die. 

normal people, when faced with an illness, are left to the decision of their family members, who, unprepared, and in a stressed situation, fall prey to the action bias. they want to do everything to save their loved ones. what they don't realise is the tradeoff. the tradeoff that while many surgeries can keep their loved ones alive, they do so only while significantly hampering the lives. which is why many people now die in hospitals, having been in ICUs for upwards of months. 

most doctors, because they see this everyday upfront, and because their work makes them think about death and how one should handle it, decide that the best situation when confronted with a terminal illness is to manage pain and die living their routine life, not tied to tubes in an ICU.

when reading the article, i was reminded of the book being mortal by atul gawande. that i read years ago, wait goodreads should have the exact date - June to July 2022 is when I read it. been over 4 years. which corroborates the fact that i barely remember anything from the book except the key takeaway that one should have the hard conversation with family membrs about how one wants to have their life end. that for most illnesses at the end of life, its better to let it be. to manage pain and just have the normal routine in the last few days. or hospice. 

i don't remember much else from the book. just another anecdote that the author shared. its only been 4 years. the books i'll read now, will my 15 year older self remember anything from it? the same could be asked about the experiences. the experiences and events that my current self places so much importance on, my 15 year older self won't even be able to recall.

events that i would probably rate the best/worst in my life until now will probably become barely-remembered memories decades from now. almost as if future me will be a different person.
