title: "thoughts: pedagogy from the lens of training vs inference"
date: 2026-11-21

i've given a bunch of exams in my academic life. we can measure exams in a 2-D space where one axis measures difficulty and the other measures conceptual clarity where one extreme is rote papers and the other extreme is pure logical papers.

i think i've given papers along the whole plane then. easy-logical, difficult-logical, easy-rote and difficult-rote.

giving exams, like everything else, is a skill. and i've come to realise that due to the lottery of birth and genes, i'm now fairly decent at it.

but i realised that for most exams what I was doing is training heavily on the training dataset. what one might call overfitting. and because the exams are from the same probability distribution, most of the times from within the training dataset, its not really difficult to fair well in exams once you know the process.

but now that my brain knows i can do that, it does not get any joy out of anymore exams. so i've decided to now test my inference capabilites instead. 

i can overfit the training dataset well and perform in exams. so the more challenging next thing to do is instead train less on the training dataset and use inference more on exams. i think a huge issue with many indian exams is that they are structured in a way and timed in a way such that only people who have overfit the training dataset will perform nicely. 

what i want to try doing now is probably just see the definition and theorem level stuff and then be able to reproduce higher findings on the exams myself. 

but also i think this is now going to be more important given the AI progress. maybe now what is a better competetive advantage is not knowing a lot of stuff in your field but having the ability to think better given that you have these tools.

obviously all this is an exercise in futile if RSI doesn't slow. then basically any strategy you make is going to be obsolete in a couple of years from now.
